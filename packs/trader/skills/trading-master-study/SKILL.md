# Trading Master Study

## When to use
- At the start (before running any backtest/paper trade) and as ongoing education. The trader learns the pros' psychology and rules BEFORE risking anything.

## The masters to learn from (all-time great day/swing traders)
- **Marty Schwartz** ("Pit Bull") — 1984 US Trading Champion. Lesson: cut losers early and often; the first cut is the cheapest.
- **Mark Minervini** (US Investing Champion, SEPA method) — selective, high-conviction entries; only buy best-of-breed setups; protect capital.
- **Oliver Velez** — pro day trader; "trading is a business, treat it like one"; risk management + discipline.
- **Andrew Aziz** (*How to Day Trade for a Living*) — mechanical execution framework; only trade the highest-probability setups.
- **Mark Douglas** (*Trading in the Zone*) — THE psychology book. Trading is probabilistic, not about being right; remove ego; manage expectation vs outcome.
- **Van K. Tharp** (*Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*) — position sizing and expectancy (R-multiples); build a system with positive expectancy.

## The core lessons (internalize these)
1. **Capital preservation first** — "the first cut is the cheapest." Cut losses early; protect the account before making money.
2. **Trading is probabilistic** — you will be wrong often. It's about expectancy over many trades, not any single trade. (Douglas)
3. **Positive expectancy** — a system wins if (win rate × avg win) > (loss rate × avg loss), after costs. (Tharp, R-multiples)
4. **Position size to the risk** — risk a fixed % per trade (never "how much will I make", ask "how much can I lose"). (Tharp)
5. **Selectivity** — most setups are not worth taking. Wait for the highest-probability setup. (Minervini, Velez)
6. **Discipline over prediction** — follow the written plan; no revenge trading, no chasing, no "this time is different".
7. **Emotion is the enemy** — fear and greed cause the biggest losses. A rule-based system removes emotion. (Douglas)

## Learning workflow
1. Read the `references/trading-masters.md` summary (key quotes + rules from each master).
2. For each master: distill 2-3 concrete, actionable rules you will actually follow.
3. Map each lesson to a rule in your risk-management skill (e.g. Douglas → strict stop-loss, no widening).
4. Before every backtest/paper trade, re-check: does this setup follow the masters' discipline?
5. Log which masters' rules you applied in each paper trade.

## Pitfalls
- Do NOT skip this study and jump to signals. The pros' edge is mostly psychology + discipline, not a secret indicator.
- Do NOT memorize quotes for show — convert each into a rule you follow.
- No single master is "the answer" — synthesize the overlapping principles (all agree: cut losses, protect capital, be selective).

## Output
- A personal "trader rules" list distilled from the masters (reference it in every signal).
- Evidence in paper-trade logs that each rule was followed.

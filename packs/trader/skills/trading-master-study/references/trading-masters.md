# Trading Masters — Key Lessons & Rules

A distilled study guide of the all-time great day/swing traders. Study these BEFORE
running any strategy. The pros' edge is mostly **psychology + discipline**, not a secret indicator.

---

## Marty Schwartz ("Pit Bull") — US Trading Champion 1984
- Grew a small account into millions by disciplined trading.
- **"The first cut is the cheapest."** Cut losing trades early; the longer you wait, the more it costs.
- Don't get attached to a position. If the trade is wrong, get out fast.
- Use a plan and follow it; let profits run only when the setup says so.
- **Rule →** hard stop-loss, always. Never average down a loser.

## Mark Minervini — US Investing Champion (SEPA method)
- Be **highly selective**: only buy the best-of-breed, highest-conviction setups. Quality over quantity.
- Protect capital at all costs; capital preservation enables you to fight another day.
- Don't trade for the sake of trading. Sit on cash when there's no good setup.
- **Rule →** fewer, better trades. Skip low-quality setups even if "something is moving."

## Oliver Velez — Pro Day Trader
- "Trading is a **business**, treat it like one." Professional, structured, disciplined.
- Risk management and discipline are the foundation; entries are secondary.
- Develop a repeatable edge and execute it consistently.
- **Rule →** treat each trade as a business decision with defined risk; no gambling.

## Andrew Aziz — *How to Day Trade for a Living*
- Trade only the **highest-probability setups**; most price action is noise.
- Have a mechanical framework: entry, stop, target, and position size defined in advance.
- Small accounts must be extra disciplined — one bad trade can hurt a lot.
- **Rule →** mechanical execution. No ad-hoc decisions mid-trade.

## Mark Douglas — *Trading in the Zone* (THE psychology book)
- Trading is **probabilistic**, not about being right. You will be wrong often — that's normal.
- Detach from each single trade's outcome; focus on **expectancy over many trades**.
- Remove ego: the market doesn't care about your opinion. Never "get even" or revenge-trade.
- Manage expectation vs outcome: a correct decision can still lose; a bad decision can still win.
- **Rule →** judge yourself by whether you followed the plan, not by the result of one trade.

## Van K. Tharp — *Trade Your Way to Financial Freedom*
- Build a system with **positive expectancy**: (win rate × avg win) > (loss rate × avg loss), after costs.
- Use **R-multiples**: measure every trade in terms of risk (R). A 2R win, a -1R loss.
- **Position size to risk**: risk a fixed % per trade, not a fixed $ that ignores stop distance.
- **Rule →** size = (risk % × capital) / (entry − stop). Never risk more than a small % per trade.

---

## The Overlapping Principles (what ALL masters agree on)
1. **Cut losses early** — the first cut is the cheapest. (Schwartz, Velez, Aziz)
2. **Protect capital first** — survive to trade another day. (Minervini, Tharp)
3. **Be selective** — only the highest-probability setups. (Minervini, Velez, Aziz)
4. **Trade probabilistically** — it's expectancy, not single-trade correctness. (Douglas, Tharp)
5. **Position-size to risk** — risk a small fixed %; never oversized. (Tharp)
6. **Discipline > prediction** — follow the plan; no revenge, no chasing, no emotion. (all)

## How to use this
- Convert each principle into a concrete rule in your **risk-management** skill.
- Reference these rules in every signal you give.
- Log in each paper trade which masters' principles you followed.
